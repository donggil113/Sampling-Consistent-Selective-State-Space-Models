\begin{abstract}
Selective state-space models compute their step size from the input rather than
from elapsed time. We ask how much of their output change under a pure change of
time grid (same physical input path) is an artifact, and which simple time-handling
choices remove it. \todo{NX1-STEM-BACKBONE: headline result on trained models}.
In a controlled single-layer toy, the combination of a dt-scaled step, an exact
zero-order-hold input matrix and a time-exact readout removes the splitting
artifact to float64 precision (FR-E1-SPLIT, FR-E3-READOUT); no new architecture is
proposed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Toy analysis (single layer, fixed parameters)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Splitting: exact ZOH is invariant (standard); Euler-$B$ first order; bilinear second order but not L-stable; $\Delta$ without $dt$ is non-convergent. Source: FR-E1-SPLIT, FR-E4-NUMERICS.
  \item New observations: with exact ZOH, the whole change is genuine path change; with $\Delta$ without $dt$, 82--89\% is artifact. Source: FR-E2-NEWOBS.
  \item Readouts under density change; the fixes do not combine additively. Source: FR-E3-READOUT.
  \item Numerical floor in float32 (emulated). Source: FR-E4-NUMERICS (N4).
  \item Caveat: toy magnitudes depend on the $\Delta|\lambda|$ scale and say nothing about trained Mamba.
\end{itemize}
